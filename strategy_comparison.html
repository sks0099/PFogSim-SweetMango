PFogSim · IMPROVED_SINGLE_LAYER_VORONOI vs. HAFA_ORCHESTRATOR

Strategy comparison across device scale, 1000–6000 devices

Full-simulation sweeps (PFogSim's native device-count loop, step 1000) comparing Voronoi — run as the dual strategy from run_dual_strategy.sh — against HAFA's own orchestrator, across every outcome and cost metric the simulator reports.

Methodology. Three full JVM sweeps, one per condition, each internally stepping 1000→6000 devices by 1000. Same topology (1074-node), same random seed, same task workload throughout. Voronoi was run under both fixed preferredHostIndexAscending_for_slv strategies — edge-first (ascending=false, searches the lowest-MIPS/densest tier first) and cloud-first (ascending=true, searches the highest-MIPS/sparsest tier first) — then, at each device count independently, a combined score of failed-task rate, average service time, and average network delay (each min–max normalized across all Voronoi runs, weighted 40/30/30 — see DUAL_STRATEGY_WEIGHTS below) picks the winner kept as the single “Voronoi (dual-strategy)” series below. Network delay is a component of service time (processing_time + network_delay = service_time exactly, every row), so scoring it separately is a deliberate double-count that gives it more say than its share within service time alone — not a statistically independent decomposition. This is a refinement over picking by failure rate alone: at 3000 devices, failure rate alone would pick cloud-first (0.58% vs 3.28% failed), but that keeps cloud-first's worst-in-dataset service time (1.187s) and network delay (1.033s); the combined score picks edge-first instead (0.827s service, 0.294s network delay). At 2000 devices, adding network delay to the score flips the pick again: failure-rate-plus-service-time alone kept cloud-first (0.57% failed), but its network delay there (0.861s) is nearly 3× edge-first's (0.292s), so the full combined score picks edge-first — accepting a jump to 5.42% failed in exchange for cutting both service time (~10%) and network delay (~66%). Orchestration-time fix (2026-08-11). Profiling assignHost() traced its per-device cost to a single line: the Voronoi BFS frontier (NeighborsOfNodesAddedToProspectiveNodes) was a plain ArrayList, so its .contains() check — called once per neighbor, against a list averaging ~768 elements — was O(N) and ran 1.32 billion times by 6000 devices, accounting for 56% of total assignHost() time on its own. Pairing it with a companion HashSet for O(1) membership checks (same pattern already used for preferredHosts/ prospectiveNodes elsewhere in this file) cut that operation's cost ~24× and dropped edge-first orchestration time at 6000 devices from 144.5ms to 59.5ms per device, with zero change to failed-task rate or any other outcome metric — verified identical at every device count before and after.
